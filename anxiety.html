Freedom From Fear & Worry

Anxiety conditions are among the most common and most treatable mental health conditions. You don't have to manage this on your own.

Anxiety is Australia's most common mental health condition, affecting around 1 in 4 people at some point in their lives. It can show up as constant worry, sudden panic, intrusive thoughts and compulsions, or a persistent sense of dread that's hard to explain.

I help you understand what's driving your anxiety and build practical, evidence-based tools to feel more in control again. Anxiety disorders cover a range of presentations, including generalised anxiety, panic disorder, social anxiety, phobias, and obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD), and they often overlap with each other. Whatever form it takes, effective, well-evidenced treatment is available.

Signs Anxiety Counselling Could Help

  • Persistent, hard to control worry about everyday things
  • Restlessness, muscle tension, or feeling constantly on edge
  • Sudden panic attacks, including a racing heart, shortness of breath, dizziness, or a fear of losing control
  • Avoiding places, situations, or activities out of fear or anxiety
  • Intrusive, unwanted thoughts that cause distress
  • Compulsions or rituals used to reduce anxiety or prevent a feared outcome
  • Difficulty sleeping due to a racing mind
  • Anxiety that's interfering with work, study, or relationships

Panic Therapy and Counselling

Panic disorder involves recurrent, unexpected panic attacks, sudden surges of intense fear accompanied by physical symptoms such as a racing heart, chest tightness, or dizziness, along with ongoing worry about having another attack. It's highly treatable, and many people fear something is physically wrong with them before recognising the pattern as panic.

OCD Therapy and Counselling

OCD involves intrusive, unwanted thoughts, images, or urges (obsessions) that cause significant distress, and repetitive behaviours or mental acts (compulsions) performed to reduce that distress or prevent a feared outcome. Common themes include contamination, harm, symmetry, or intrusive taboo thoughts. OCD responds well to structured, evidence-based treatment.

Anxiety, OCD & Panic FAQs

Anxiety counselling is professional support for generalised anxiety, panic disorder, phobias, social anxiety, and obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD). It helps identify what's driving the anxiety and build practical, evidence-based tools to feel more in control.

Common signs include persistent, hard to control worry, restlessness or muscle tension, avoidance of feared situations, difficulty sleeping due to a racing mind, and anxiety that's interfering with work, study, or relationships.

A panic attack is a sudden surge of intense fear accompanied by physical symptoms such as a racing heart, shortness of breath, chest tightness, or dizziness, typically peaking within minutes. Panic disorder involves recurrent, unexpected panic attacks along with ongoing worry about having another one.

Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD) involves intrusive, unwanted thoughts, images, or urges (obsessions) that cause distress, and repetitive behaviours or mental acts (compulsions) performed to reduce that distress or prevent a feared outcome. Common themes include contamination, harm, symmetry, or intrusive taboo thoughts.

The most well-evidenced approach to OCD is Cognitive Behavioural Therapy that includes gradual, structured exposure work, often combined with Acceptance and Commitment Therapy or Schema Therapy where longstanding patterns are involved. Treatment focuses on steadily reducing the power of obsessions and compulsions through this structured, supported approach.

Yes. Cognitive Behavioural Therapy and related psychotherapies are well evidenced treatments for anxiety, panic disorder, and OCD on their own. Some people benefit from combining therapy with medication prescribed by a GP or psychiatrist, which is a decision best made with your treating professionals.
