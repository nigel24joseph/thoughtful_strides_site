A Space to Work Through It Together

Not feeling as connected as you used to?

In my work with couples, I often hear how it was in the beginning. You were friends, you enjoyed each other's company, you went on dates and did things together, and you made each other a priority. For some couples, a few unhelpful ways of relating were there from the start, but they felt manageable while the relationship was new.

As life goes on, family responsibilities, financial pressures, work stress, and health issues can make it harder to keep doing what once came easily. Time together shrinks, and you get caught up in life. When that happens, there's very little emotional connection in the bank to carry you through disagreements, and things can start to feel difficult and painful.

As your couples therapist, I offer marriage and relationship counselling as a space to understand what's changed in your relationship and find the best way forward, together.

Where Couples Are When They Arrive

Wherever You Are, It's Worth Starting

In my work with couples, I've noticed most arrive at one of four places.

Which sounds most like you?

Noticing early

Some couples notice things have changed and reach out straight away. Because not much hurt has built up yet, the road back to feeling close is often shorter and easier.

You might notice

  • Feeling less connected
  • Less time together
  • Small arguments happening more often

Stuck in the same arguments

For other couples, things haven't been right for a while. You may be caught in the same arguments, drifting apart, or carrying hurt and resentment that has built up over time. The road is longer and asks for real commitment from both of you, but these patterns can change, and many couples get back on track.

You might notice

  • Ongoing conflict
  • Feeling unheard
  • Loss of closeness or intimacy
  • Differences in values or goals

After an affair

Some couples come to counselling after an affair or another betrayal of trust has come to light. It can feel like the ground has been pulled out from under you. Recovery is possible, and it happens in stages: facing what happened, understanding what was missing, and slowly rebuilding closeness and trust. Many couples come out of it with a stronger, more honest relationship.

You might notice

  • Infidelity
  • Broken trust
  • Secrets or lies
  • Not knowing if you can move forward

At the crossroads

Some couples come as a last step, wanting to know they've tried everything. Sometimes both partners feel close to the end. Sometimes one has been feeling this way for a while and the other has only just found out. Even here, I've seen couples turn things around. For others, counselling helps them make a clear decision and part with more understanding and respect.

You might notice

  • Thinking about separating
  • One partner wanting to leave
  • Feeling there is nothing left to try

Wherever you are, part of our early work together is understanding where each of you stands and what you each hope for, so we can find the right way forward. Contact me to get started.

Emotionally Focused Couples Therapy

An attachment-based approach that slows the defensive cycle down so you can see what's underneath it, often hurt, fear, or a need to feel close again.

Rather than focusing on the argument itself, EFCT looks at the emotional cycle underneath it, the moment one of you reaches for connection and the other pulls back, or one raises their voice and the other shuts down. Tap through the five tools below to see how each one works.

Looks at the underlying emotional fears driving conflict, rather than just the conflict itself. Most conflict is really about needing to feel wanted, safe, or important to the other person.

Both partners' defensive reactions feed off each other. In session, I help you both slow that cycle down before it escalates. Once you can name the cycle together, it becomes something you're both working against, rather than each other.

Once the cycle settles, we look at what's underneath it, often hurt, fear, or a need to feel close again. Being able to show that to each other, and have it land gently, is often what actually shifts things.

Learning to recognise the small moments you're each reaching for reassurance or closeness, even when it looks like anger or distance, so you can respond to what's actually being asked for.

Creating new, consistent experiences of being there for each other, so trust is rebuilt through repeated action over time, not just through talking about it.

When Old Hurts Get in the Way

EMDR Therapy in Couples Counselling

Sometimes the same argument keeps going round in circles, not because of what's happening now, but because it touches something older. A tone of voice, a look, or feeling dismissed can bring up hurts from childhood, past relationships, or the shock of a betrayal. When that happens, it's hard to stay calm and connected, even when you both want to.

When EMDR may help

  • Reactions bigger than the moment. Feeling flooded, panicked, or shutting down in ways that echo older hurts.
  • After an affair or betrayal. Replaying images, constant checking, or being pulled back to the moment you found out.
  • Painful beliefs. Thoughts like "I'm not enough" or "I'll be left" that make it hard to reach for each other.
  • Shared losses. Miscarriage, infertility, or the death of a loved one, grieved differently by each of you.
  • Closeness and intimacy. Physical or emotional closeness that brings up past experiences.

How EMDR helps

EMDR (Eye Movement Desensitisation and Reprocessing) helps process those older memories so they lose their intensity. You stay in the present with your partner instead of being pulled back into the past, which makes it easier to listen, stay calm, and reach for each other again.

EMDR is done in individual sessions, alongside our couples work. We always talk it through together first, and only use EMDR when it feels right for you both.

If there is fear, control, or violence in the relationship, safety comes first, and EMDR isn't the right starting point.
