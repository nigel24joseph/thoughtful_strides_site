Protect What Matters

Anger is a normal, healthy emotion, but when it shows up too often, too intensely, or in ways that hurt the people or work you care about, it's time to reach out and change it. Often, anger that feels impossible to control traces back further than the moment itself, sometimes to childhood experiences of powerlessness or not being able to protect yourself or someone you loved.

Anger is rarely the problem itself. It is the signal that something underneath it still needs attention.

If you often feel tense, on edge, or braced for something to go wrong, that heightened state is frequently a trauma response too, and it's genuinely hard to shift without support. EMDR works directly with the trauma underneath the reactivity, not just the anger on the surface.

My approach is not just to help you find ways to manage your anger better, but to deal with the causes of your anger, so the changes can be longer term.

Signs Anger Management Counselling Could Help

  • Frequent irritability, snapping, or losing your temper over small things
  • Angry outbursts you regret afterwards, with partners, children, colleagues, or strangers
  • Feeling like your anger comes on fast and is hard to control once it starts
  • Using anger to avoid feeling more vulnerable emotions
  • Relationship, family, or workplace conflict linked to how you express frustration
  • A history of trauma, high stress upbringing, or unresolved resentment feeding current reactions

Anger Management Therapy and Counselling

In Anger Management Counselling, I take the time to get to know you. I want to understand what you've been through and what your hopes from counselling are. We will then work through strategies to make changes and how we can implement and sustain those changes permanently.

1

Getting to Know You

In our first sessions, I want to understand your history and life experience growing up, when you first noticed your anger, and how it's shaped your life and continues to impact you.

I'll also ask what you've already tried, so we're not covering the same ground again. And we'll talk about where anger's hit hardest, whether that's your relationships, work, or family, so we focus on what matters most to you.

2

Developing Strategies

Anger is rarely the whole story. Together we look at what's underneath it, the hurt, fear, shame, or unmet needs driving it, and start building strategies to catch it early and calm your nervous system in the moment.

I draw on approaches like CBT and Schema Therapy to help make sense of the patterns underneath your anger. Where past trauma or a high stress upbringing is feeding your reactions, I also draw on EMDR to help process what's keeping your nervous system on high alert. From here, we start practising ways to recognise escalation before it takes over, and communicate frustration without it damaging the relationships that matter to you.

Read more ↓
3

Consolidating the Changes

This stage is about making the change last, not just understanding it, but actually living it. We consolidate what you've learned, and where anger has affected trust or relationships, we work on repairing that too.

This is about becoming the person you actually want to be when it matters, steady and settled rather than reactive, using what you've learned in real situations rather than just talking about it. It's less about managing anger and more about adjusting to a different way of living, one where you don't need anger to feel heard, safe, or in control. Where relevant, it also means repairing trust in relationships affected by anger in the past.
