My Story

  1. Early 1990s

    Migrating to Australia

    My path to becoming a trauma therapist and couples counsellor didn't start in a consulting room. After migrating to Australia in the early 1990s, I spent around ten years working as an electronics technician, a stable career, but never quite the right fit. I have always been drawn to people, to understanding what shapes us, what wounds us, and what helps us heal. That pull eventually became impossible to ignore.

  2. Community Health, Melbourne

    Seeing the impact of early trauma

    My early years were in Community Health in Melbourne, supporting people with addiction and mental illness. Almost without exception, early childhood trauma was part of their story. For some it was abuse, assault or family violence. For others it was quieter: emotional neglect, criticism, or never quite feeling seen or safe. Either way, the impact on their adult lives was profound.

    I knew that if I was going to truly help people, I needed to understand trauma at a much deeper level.

  3. Trauma training and EMDR

    Understanding trauma more deeply

    Since then, I have trained extensively with leading trauma specialists, attended international trauma conferences, and continued to study the research that is transforming how we understand and treat trauma.

    One of the things I noticed early in my work was how painful it could be for clients to talk about their experiences. Revisiting the past often left people feeling worse, not better. EMDR offered something different, a way to process and heal from traumatic memories without needing to retell the story.

  4. Private practice

    Working with couples

    I worked with couples from time to time throughout my career and kept seeing the same thing: each partner brings their own history into the relationship, and that history shapes how they argue and how they connect. Since starting my private practice, couples work has become a central part of what I do.

    In conflict, each person is usually protecting something, a fear of not mattering or of being left, and each reaction sets off the other. I use the Gottman Method and Emotionally Focused Couples Therapy to slow that cycle down and reach what sits underneath it, with individual EMDR sessions where old wounds keep getting in the way.
