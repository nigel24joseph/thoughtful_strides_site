Trauma Therapy and Counselling

Before we start Trauma Therapy, I take the time to get to know you. I want to understand what you've been through, and what needs to change for you to have a fulfilling life. We identify the experiences that are still affecting you, without you having to talk about them in detail. Trauma therapy is a three stage process: Safety and Stabilisation, Trauma Memory Processing, and Integration.

1

Safety and Stabilisation

I'll help you with strategies to regulate your emotions and manage distress, so you feel safe.

We'll work together on building a stronger sense of control over your day to day life and environment. This stage takes as long as it needs to, I won't move you into the next stage until you're ready.

Trauma Memory Processing

I use EMDR to help you process the trauma memories, along with the beliefs and emotions tied to them.

You won't need to describe what happened in detail, you simply bring the memory to mind while I use eye movements to help your brain process it. I'll usually see you more often during this stage, so you always have support close by as new memories or feelings come up between sessions.

EMDR Therapy and Counselling

I'll ask you to bring up an image in your mind of what happened. Then we identify the negative belief about yourself connected to it, along with the feelings and sensations in your body. After that, I'll ask you to move your eyes from left to right.

We repeat this over several sets of eye movements. Over time, the image becomes blurry, harder to recall, or changes altogether. The emotional charge settles, the negative belief no longer feels true, and the body sensations tied to it are gone. I'd consider this a processed memory.

Because memories tend to be stored in themes, you won't need every single memory processed to start feeling better.

PTSD Therapy and Counselling

Traumatic memories often become fragmented because of how intense the distress was at the time, and I use EMDR to process these fragmented pieces.

Parts of the memory can get stored separately, things like the emotions, sensory pieces such as sound, touch, smell or images, and negative beliefs about yourself. These pieces can surface unpredictably, which is closely linked to the symptoms of PTSD. It can show up as flashbacks, nightmares, or a sense of being back "in" the moment, even years later.

Sometimes it can show up as just sensations or nightmares without any memory of what happened, EMDR can process these memories too that are attached to these sensations.

Complex Trauma and Complex PTSD Therapy

Complex trauma and Complex PTSD counselling follows the same three stage process. Each stage can take longer, depending on your own circumstances and history. I use EMDR to process the trauma memories that are still driving distress and reactivity. Alongside this, I use Schema Therapy to work through the beliefs and coping patterns that helped you survive at the time, but aren't serving you now.

Schema Therapy

Schemas are deep patterns, made up of memories, emotions, and beliefs about yourself and others, that formed early in life. I'll ask you to fill out a questionnaire to identify your schemas.

Schemas develop when your need to feel safe, supported, or free to be yourself wasn't fully met growing up. Even though you're an adult now, these patterns tend to keep repeating. They shape how you see yourself, and how you react in relationships, work, and everyday life. Schemas help us trace back to the memories from complex trauma that shaped them, so we know what to target in processing.

Trauma Therapy FAQs

Trauma is not defined by the event itself, it is what happens to you when your nervous system is overwhelmed beyond its capacity to cope. It is less about what happened to you, and more about what happened inside you. When an experience leaves you feeling unsafe, helpless, or utterly alone, and your nervous system cannot fully process and recover from it, that is trauma.

Signs of trauma can include:

  • Flashbacks, intrusive memories, or distressing dreams
  • Feeling on edge, hypervigilant, or easily startled
  • Emotional numbness, disconnection, or feeling detached from life
  • Difficulty trusting others or feeling safe in relationships
  • Anxiety, depression, or unexplained physical symptoms
  • Shame, guilt, or a persistent sense that something is wrong with you
  • Avoiding people, places, or situations that trigger distressing feelings
  • A feeling that you have never quite felt safe, settled, or fully yourself

Big T traumas are the experiences most people associate with the word, accidents, assault, abuse, natural disasters, war, or sudden loss. These are significant events that threaten safety or survival and can leave a lasting imprint on the nervous system. Little t traumas are less visible but equally real, childhood emotional neglect, chronic criticism, bullying, relationship breakdowns, or years of feeling like you didn't quite belong. They rarely involve a single dramatic moment, and the nervous system doesn't distinguish the size of the threat when deciding whether it was overwhelming, which is why little t traumas so often go unrecognised, even by the person carrying them.

Unresolved trauma doesn't stay contained to the individual, it moves into your closest connections. Arguments that escalate too quickly, silences that go on too long, or feeling fundamentally unseen by the person who's supposed to know you best, are often trauma responses playing out between two people. When you feel threatened or emotionally overwhelmed, you react from old survival patterns rather than your best self. This is one of the reasons couples counselling and individual trauma work often benefit from happening alongside each other.

Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD) can develop after experiencing or witnessing a traumatic event such as an accident, assault, abuse, natural disaster, military combat, or another life-threatening experience. Trauma can disrupt how memories are stored, sometimes resulting in memory fragmentation, where parts of the experience remain disconnected rather than forming a coherent narrative.

People with PTSD may experience intrusive memories, flashbacks, nightmares, anxiety, emotional numbness, sleep difficulties, irritability, or a persistent sense of danger. PTSD is a treatable condition, and evidence-based therapies can help process traumatic memories and restore a greater sense of safety and control.
