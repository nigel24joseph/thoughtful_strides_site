A Space to Grieve in Your Own Way

Losing someone can touch every part of your life: your thoughts, feelings, beliefs, physical health, and relationships with others.

I offer a non-judgemental space to talk through your relationship, what that person meant to you, what life looks like now that they're gone, and how you find your way forward without them. Whether you're facing a recent bereavement or a loss from years ago, we go at the pace that feels right for you.

More Than One Kind of Loss

Grief is a natural response to loss: the death of someone close, but also the loss of a relationship, health, identity, a pet, a pregnancy, or a future you expected. When several losses come close together, the weight can build up and feel harder to name than grief from a single loss.

I can support you through all forms of grief and loss, in a way that fits you and how you experience it.

Grief has no fixed timeline, and it can show up as sadness, anger, guilt, relief, numbness, or a mix of all of these, sometimes all in the same day. My role is to walk alongside you as you find your own way through it.

When to Reach Out

Signs Grief Counselling Could Help

There's no right or wrong time to ask for support. If some of these feel familiar, it may help to talk it through with someone.

  • Grief that feels stuck

    Still as intense as it was months or years ago

  • Avoiding or consumed

    Avoiding reminders, or unable to think of anything else

  • Hard to accept

    Struggling to accept that the loss is real

  • Feelings not easing

    Guilt, anger or yearning that stays strong

  • Pulling back

    From people, work or things you used to enjoy

  • An unrecognised loss

    A loss others don't acknowledge or support

  • Space to process

    Unhurried time, even long after the loss

  • A sudden or traumatic loss

    Shock that's hard to move past

  • Sleep and appetite

    Grief showing up in your body and routine

Over Time

The grief circle never gets smaller. What you lost stays just as significant. But over time your life grows around it, new experiences, new people, moments of joy again.

Because your life is bigger, grief takes up less of your day to day, even though it's still entirely there (Tonkin, 1996).

Every Loss Is Different

Forms of Grief and Loss I Support

Grief takes many forms, and not all of them follow a death. Here's how they tend to group.

Loss Through Death

Bereavement

The death of a parent, partner, child, sibling, friend, or other loved one.

Sudden or Traumatic Loss

Sudden, traumatic, or violent loss, including suicide bereavement.

Anticipatory Grief

Grieving in advance of an expected death or decline.

Complicated Grief

Complicated or prolonged grief that hasn't eased with time.

Loss Beyond Death

Disenfranchised Grief

Losses not widely recognised or socially supported, such as miscarriage or pet loss.

Ambiguous Loss

Loss without closure, such as estrangement, dementia, or a missing person.

Non-Death Loss

Divorce, infertility, chronic illness, job loss, or loss of identity and independence.

Cumulative Grief

Compounded grief from multiple losses.

When Grief Gets Stuck

EMDR Therapy for Grief and Loss

For most people, grief softens gradually over time, even though it never fully goes away. For some, it stays just as raw months or years later, and starts to get in the way of daily life. This isn't a sign you're grieving wrong. Often, something is keeping the grief stuck.

That something can be painful memories tied to the loss: how they died, the last images of them, the phone call, the hospital room, or the "what ifs" and guilt that keep playing over. When these memories stay raw, every time you think of your loved one you're pulled back into the moment of loss, instead of being able to remember who they were to you.

How EMDR helps

EMDR (Eye Movement Desensitisation and Reprocessing) helps your mind process these memories so they lose their intensity. It doesn't take away your grief or your love for the person. It gently loosens the painful moments that are blocking it, so grief can move naturally again. Many people find they can think of their loved one with warmth, and recall happier memories that had been hard to reach.

I'm trained in EMDR Therapy and use it where a loss has been sudden, traumatic, or simply too painful to process, always at a pace that feels safe for you. If the loss has stirred up older trauma too, trauma therapy can sit alongside our grief work.

Grief is not something to get over. It is something you learn to carry differently, until it becomes part of how you move forward rather than what holds you back.

Is EMDR Safe?

EMDR is well researched, and how it's delivered makes the difference. Most difficulties come from starting memory work before someone feels steady enough, choosing the wrong memories to work on, missing signs of overwhelm, or ending a session without settling things. I take time to prepare you, pace each session to what you can manage, and make sure you leave settled.
