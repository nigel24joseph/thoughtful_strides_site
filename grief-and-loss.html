A Space to Grieve in Your Own Way

Losing someone can touch every part of your life: your thoughts, feelings, beliefs, physical health, and relationships with others.

I offer a non-judgemental space to talk through your relationship, what that person meant to you, what life looks like now that they're gone, and how you find your way forward without them.

More Than One Kind of Loss

Grief is a natural response to loss, most often the death of someone close to us, but also the loss of a relationship, health, identity, a pet, a pregnancy, or a future that was expected but didn't happen. Sometimes grief builds up over time too, especially when several losses happen close together or you haven't had the chance to fully process one before the next arrives, and that cumulative weight can feel harder to place than grief from a single loss.

I can support you through all forms of grief and loss, in a way that fits you and how you experience it.

There is no single right way to grieve, and no fixed timeline. Grief can show up as sadness, anger, guilt, relief, numbness, or a mix of all of these, sometimes all in the same day. My role is to walk alongside you as you find your own way through it.

Signs Grief Counselling Could Help

  • Grief that feels stuck, or as intense today as it was months or years ago
  • Avoiding reminders of the loss, or being unable to think about anything else
  • Difficulty accepting the reality of the loss
  • Intense guilt, anger, or yearning that isn't easing
  • Withdrawing from relationships, work, or activities you used to enjoy
  • A loss that isn't being acknowledged or supported by the people around you
  • Wanting a safe, unhurried space to process a loss, even a long time after it happened

Early in Grief

Over Time

The grief circle never gets smaller. What you lost stays just as significant. But over time your life grows around it, new experiences, new people, moments of joy again.

Because your life is bigger, grief takes up less of your day to day, even though it's still entirely there.

Forms of Grief and Loss I Support

Grief takes many forms, not all of them following a death. Here's how they tend to group.

Loss Through Death

Bereavement

The death of a parent, partner, child, sibling, friend, or other loved one.

Sudden or Traumatic Loss

Sudden, traumatic, or violent loss, including suicide bereavement.

Anticipatory Grief

Grieving in advance of an expected death or decline.

Complicated Grief

Complicated or prolonged grief that hasn't eased with time.

Loss Beyond Death

Disenfranchised Grief

Losses not widely recognised or socially supported, such as miscarriage, pet loss, or estrangement.

Ambiguous Loss

Loss without closure, such as estrangement, dementia, or a missing person.

Non-Death Loss

Divorce, infertility, chronic illness, job loss, or loss of identity and independence.

Cumulative Grief

Compounded grief from multiple losses.

When Grief Becomes Complicated

For most people, grief eases gradually over time, even though it never fully disappears. For some, grief stays just as intense for much longer than expected, and starts to get in the way of daily life. This isn't a sign of doing grief wrong. It's something the right support can make a real difference with.

Sometimes a loss carries its own trauma: a sudden or violent death, or last images that sit closer to trauma memories than to the person you knew. When that happens, grief can get interrupted every time you think of them, pulled back into the moment of loss instead of being able to sit with who they were to you. This is often part of what makes grief complicated or prolonged. I'm trained in EMDR Therapy, and where trauma is tangled up with a loss like this, I use it to help process those traumatic memories, so grief can move again and you can remember your loved one fondly rather than being pulled back to how they died.

Grief is not something to get over. It is something you learn to carry differently, until it becomes part of how you move forward rather than what holds you back.
