You can be good at the job and still carry it home

Your work puts you in situations most people never face. Over time, one incident, or many of them building up, can start to show up in your sleep, your mood, and how close you feel to the people at home.

You can process a traumatic event without having to talk about it.

I'm a trauma therapist, and I've used EMDR for 12 years to help people with all kinds of trauma. The same approach works for trauma that comes from frontline work. Counselling here is outside your agency, and nothing is shared with your workplace without your consent.

Working With a Trauma Counsellor

Before we start, I take the time to understand what you've been through and what you want to change. We go at a pace that keeps you able to work and function day to day.

1

Safety and Stabilisation

Practical strategies for sleep, switching off after shift and managing distress, so you feel more in control first.

2

Trauma Memory Processing

I use EMDR to process the incidents that keep coming back, along with the beliefs and emotions tied to them.

3

Integration

Rebuilding confidence at work and connection at home, and planning a safe return to work if you're off.

We will move back and forth through these stages as needed.

EMDR for Single Incidents

When one incident stands out, we focus on that memory directly. You bring it to mind briefly while I guide eye movements, without needing to describe it in detail.

Over several sets, the image usually becomes less vivid, the emotional charge settles, and the negative belief connected to it no longer feels true.

EMDR for Cumulative Trauma

Many people in frontline roles aren't troubled by one incident, but by years of them. EMDR can work with that build up too.

Memories tend to be stored in themes, so we focus on the incidents that still carry the most charge. Processing those often eases the wider build up, without working through every single one.

Is EMDR Safe?

EMDR is well researched, and how it's delivered makes the difference. Most difficulties come from starting memory work before someone feels steady enough, choosing the wrong memories to work on, missing signs of overwhelm, or ending a session without settling things. I take time to prepare you, pace each session to what you can manage, and make sure you leave settled.
