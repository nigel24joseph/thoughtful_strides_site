Reconnect With Life

When everything feels heavier than it should, getting through the day can start to feel like an effort in itself. Depression can show up as a flat, disconnected feeling, or as exhaustion that sleep doesn't fix, or as a quiet sense that the things you once enjoyed have lost their pull.

If that sounds familiar, you are not alone, and it is not a sign of weakness. It is a sign that something needs support.

Depression often narrows life down to survival. Therapy is about widening it back out again, one workable step at a time.

What are the signs of depression?

  • A persistent low or flat mood, most of the day, most days
  • Losing interest or pleasure in things you used to enjoy
  • Fatigue or low energy that doesn't improve with rest
  • Changes in sleep, sleeping far more or far less than usual
  • Changes in appetite or weight
  • Difficulty concentrating or making decisions
  • Feelings of worthlessness, guilt, or being a burden
  • Withdrawing from family, friends, or activities
  • A sense of hopelessness about the future

What Causes Depression?

Depression rarely has a single cause. It often develops through a combination of biological factors, life circumstances, and psychological patterns built up over time. Grief and loss, chronic stress, unresolved trauma, relationship difficulties, major life transitions, and longstanding beliefs about yourself can all play a part. Depression following a significant loss can benefit from grief and loss counselling, depression connected to relationship strain from relationship counselling, and where trauma is underneath it, trauma therapy and EMDR can help.

How Does Therapy Help?

Therapy helps you understand what is driving your depression, rather than just managing the symptoms day to day. Together we look at the thoughts, patterns, and circumstances that keep low mood in place, and build a path toward feeling more like yourself again. This is not about forcing positivity. It is about understanding what is happening and finding a way through it that actually fits your life.

When Low Mood Has Deeper Roots

EMDR Therapy for Depression

Depression is often held in place by beliefs that formed long before the low mood started. Thoughts like "I'm a failure" or "nothing I do matters" can feel like facts, especially when they're tied to earlier experiences of loss, criticism, or feeling unwanted. Talking about them helps, but sometimes the feeling underneath stays the same.

When EMDR may help

  • Harsh beliefs about yourself. A constant sense of not being good enough, however much you achieve.
  • Low mood after something difficult. Depression that started after a loss, a breakup, a job ending, or a frightening event.
  • Childhood experiences. Growing up with criticism, neglect, or having to look after yourself too early.
  • Shame and guilt. Replaying past mistakes or feeling that something is wrong with you.
  • Depression that keeps coming back. Feeling better for a while, then sliding back into the same place.

How EMDR helps

EMDR (Eye Movement Desensitisation and Reprocessing) works with the memories that keep those beliefs alive. As the memories are processed, the beliefs lose their weight, and a kinder, more balanced view of yourself has room to grow. Many people notice more energy and hope as this happens.

We go at a pace that feels manageable. Before any EMDR processing, we spend time building steadiness and support, so you have more to draw on when things feel heavy.

If you're having thoughts of ending your life, your safety comes first, and we focus on support before any EMDR work. If you need help right now, call Lifeline on 13 11 14 or 000 in an emergency.
