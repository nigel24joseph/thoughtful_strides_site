Feel Close to the People You Love

Many of the difficulties people experience in relationships have less to do with the other person than with patterns formed long before that relationship began, ways of relating, trusting, and responding to conflict that were shaped early in life and tend to repeat themselves.

This individual counselling is for anyone wanting to understand their own part in relationship difficulties, whether they're currently in a relationship, between relationships, or simply noticing a pattern they'd like to change.

This work is separate from couples counselling. It's one-on-one, focused on you, your history, and your patterns, and can be valuable whether or not you have a partner right now.

Signs Relationship Counselling Could Help

  • A pattern of relationships ending the same way, or for similar reasons
  • Difficulty trusting partners, or a fear of being left or betrayed
  • Feeling anxious, preoccupied, or overly reliant on reassurance in relationships
  • Avoiding closeness, or feeling smothered when a relationship becomes serious
  • Repeating dynamics from your family of origin in adult relationships
  • Difficulty communicating needs, or expressing them in ways that push people away
  • Struggling to set boundaries, or feeling responsible for others' emotions
  • Wanting to understand your patterns before entering a new relationship

Understanding Attachment Style

Attachment theory describes how early relationships with caregivers shape the templates we later bring into adult relationships. Someone with a more anxious attachment style may seek frequent reassurance and fear abandonment. Someone with a more avoidant style may feel uneasy with closeness and pull away when a relationship deepens. A more secure attachment style tends to involve comfort with both closeness and independence. Most people show a mix of these patterns rather than fitting neatly into one category, and patterns can look different depending on the relationship.

Importantly, attachment style is not fixed. While early patterns can be persistent, research indicates they can shift over time, particularly through new relational experiences and focused therapeutic work.

The relationships that shaped you early in life do not have to determine the relationships you have from here.

Relationship Therapy and Counselling

This work doesn't follow a script, but sessions tend to move through a few natural stages as we get to know your patterns and build the capacity to relate differently.

1

Understanding Your Patterns

In our first sessions, I want to understand your attachment style, where it comes from, and the patterns that keep showing up across your relationships.

We look at your history and family of origin dynamics, using Schema Therapy to understand the patterns still shaping how you connect today, and how they show up differently depending on the relationship.

2

Working Through What's Underneath

Together we work through past relational hurt or betrayal, and build your tolerance for the closeness or independence that feels hardest for you.

Where past experiences are still shaping how you connect, I also draw on EMDR to help process what's keeping old patterns in place, alongside Acceptance and Commitment Therapy to build your tolerance for the discomfort of vulnerability and connection.

3

Building Healthier Connection

We build practical communication and boundary setting skills, so you can approach relationships, current or future, with greater self-awareness and steadiness.

This is practical, goal-focused work, real skills you can use, not just insight. Where you're currently in a relationship, this is separate from couples counselling, but the two can also work well together.
