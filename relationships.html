Feel Close to the People You Love

Many relationship difficulties have less to do with the other person than with patterns formed long before, ways of trusting and handling conflict shaped early in life.

This counselling is for anyone wanting to understand their part, whether you're in a relationship, between relationships, or noticing a pattern you'd like to change.

This work is separate from couples counselling. It's one-on-one, focused on you, your history, and your patterns, and can be valuable whether or not you have a partner right now.

Understanding Attachment Style

Attachment theory describes how early relationships with caregivers shape what we bring into adult relationships. Anxious attachment can mean seeking reassurance and fearing abandonment. Avoidant attachment can mean pulling away as closeness grows. Secure attachment means being comfortable with both closeness and independence. Most people show a mix.

Importantly, attachment style is not fixed. While early patterns can be persistent, research indicates they can shift over time, particularly through new relational experiences and focused therapeutic work.

The relationships that shaped you early in life do not have to determine the relationships you have from here.

When Old Patterns Keep Repeating

EMDR Therapy for Relationship Patterns

The way we connect with others is shaped early in life. If closeness once felt unsafe, unpredictable, or conditional, those lessons can follow you into adult relationships. You might find yourself in the same painful patterns with different people, even when you can see them happening.

When EMDR may help

  • Fear of abandonment. Worrying that people will leave, or needing constant reassurance.
  • Pulling away when things get close. Shutting down or keeping people at a distance to stay safe.
  • Hurt from past relationships. A betrayal or breakup that still affects how much you can trust.
  • People pleasing. Putting others first and finding it hard to say what you need.
  • Painful beliefs. Thoughts like "I'm too much", "I'm unlovable", or "I'll be let down".

How EMDR helps

EMDR (Eye Movement Desensitisation and Reprocessing) helps process the early experiences that shaped how you attach to others. As those memories lose their intensity, you can feel safer in closeness, notice old patterns sooner, and choose how you want to respond.

This work is done in individual sessions. If you and your partner would like to work on things together, couples counselling may also be an option.

If you're in a relationship where there's fear, control, or violence, your safety comes first, and we focus on that before EMDR processing.

Is EMDR Safe?

EMDR is well researched, and how it's delivered makes the difference. Most difficulties come from starting memory work before someone feels steady enough, choosing the wrong memories to work on, missing signs of overwhelm, or ending a session without settling things. I take time to prepare you, pace each session to what you can manage, and make sure you leave settled.
